Boolean logic

Foundations AND · OR · NOT · XOR · truth table out 1

Boolean logic deals in true and false—usually written 1 and 0, the same symbols as binary bits. Gates combine those bits: AND needs both on, OR needs either, NOT flips one wire, XOR is “exactly one.”

The four gates

  • AND 1 only if both inputs are 1 A ∧ B
  • OR 1 if either input is 1 A ∨ B
  • NOT flips a single input ¬A
  • XOR 1 if the inputs differ A ⊕ B

Why it matters

Every digital chip is a network of these decisions. Comparisons, masks, branch conditions, and ALUs all bottom out in Boolean operations on bits—see bit manipulation for whole-byte AND/OR/XOR and shifts.

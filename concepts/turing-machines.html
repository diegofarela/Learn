Turing machines

Theory tape · state · transition step 0

A Turing machine is the standard unlimited model of computation: finite control, a read/write tape, and a head that moves left/right. Finite automata only move right and cannot rewrite; TMs can simulate any algorithm and underpin decidability and complexity ( P vs NP).

When this is useful

  • Mental model of what “computable” means when scoping an automation feature.
  • Recognizing undecidable asks (“will this script ever finish?”) in product design.
  • Foundation for complexity talk before promising perfect static analysis.

What a TM has

        

          Finite states, a tape alphabet (with blank), a transition function, a
          start state, and accept/reject states. Configurations are
          (state, tape contents, head position).
        

        
Why it matters

        

          Church–Turing thesis: anything intuitively computable is TM-computable.
          Limits of TMs are limits of algorithms—halting is undecidable; time
          bounds define complexity classes.

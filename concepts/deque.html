Deque

Data structures double-ended · push / pop front & rear · O(1) size 3

A deque (double-ended queue) lets you insert and remove at both ends. It is the flexible middle ground between a stack (one end only) and a queue (add one end, remove the other).

The rule

        

          Both ends are open. That is the whole abstract data type: you still
          never dig into the middle, but you choose which end for every insert
          and remove. Under the hood a deque is often a
          circular array
          (wrap indices with modulo) or a
          doubly linked list
          — same double-ended ops, different guts. The demo is the circular-array
          version.

Stack vs queue vs deque

        
    
          
  • 
            Stack
            — push and pop at one end only (LIFO)
          
    • 
          
  • 
            Queue
            — enqueue at rear, dequeue at front (FIFO)
          
    • 
          
  • 
            Deque — all four end operations; can emulate either
            stack or queue
          
    • 
        


        
Where deques show up

        
    
          
  • 
            Sliding-window algorithms — maintain candidates at
            both ends of a window
          
    • 
          
  • 
            Undo / redo — history that grows and shrinks from
            either side of a cursor
          
    • 
          
  • 
            Work stealing — threads push locally and steal from
            the other end of a deque
          
    • 
          
  • 
            Palindrome checks — compare and discard from both
            ends toward the middle

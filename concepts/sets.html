Sets

Data structures unique · membership · no duplicates |A| 3

A set stores distinct values—membership, not order or multiplicity. Ask “is x in S?”; add and remove change who belongs. Under the hood, languages often implement sets with hash tables (or trees) so membership stays near constant time.

Uniqueness

        

          Adding an element that is already present does nothing to the
          cardinality. Sets answer membership, not “how many times.” That is
          the difference from a list or multiset.
        


        
Union & intersection

        

          Union A ∪ B is everything in A or B (still unique).
          Intersection A ∩ B is only what appears in both.
          Those are the algebra of sets—same ideas as in discrete math.

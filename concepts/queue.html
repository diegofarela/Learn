Queue

Data structures FIFO · enqueue / dequeue / front · O(1) size 3

A queue is a collection you touch at both ends: enqueue adds at the rear, dequeue removes from the front. First in is first out—like a line at a ticket window, or the work list behind breadth-first search.

The rule

        

          Access is restricted to opposite ends. That is the whole abstract data
          type: you never cut the line. Under the hood a queue is often a
          circular array
          (wrap indices with modulo) or a
          linked list
          (add at the tail, remove at the head)—same FIFO, different guts. The
          demo on the right is the circular-array version.

Where queues show up

        
    
          
  • 
            Breadth-first search
            — expand layer by layer; neighbors wait in a queue
          
    • 
          
  • 
            Print spooler — jobs line up; the printer takes the
            oldest first
          
    • 
          
  • 
            Message queues — producers enqueue work; consumers
            dequeue it
          
    • 
          
  • 
            Event loops — UI and I/O events wait in order until
            handled
          
    • 
          
  • 
            Task scheduling — ready processes or requests served
            FIFO
          
    • 
        


        

          Contrast with a
          stack: last in, first
          out—same “list with rules,” but both ops hit one end instead of opposite
          ends.
        


        

          
For mastery

          
    
            
  • 
              Invariant
              FIFO: enqueue at the rear, dequeue at the front; order of arrival
              is order of departure.
            
    • 
            
  • 
              When / when not
              Use for
              BFS, buffering, and
              fair scheduling. Not for LIFO undo (use a
              stack) or
              priority service (use a
              heap /
              priority queue).
            
    • 
            
  • 
              Common failure
              Mixing up front and rear; circular-buffer wrap bugs; dequeue on
              empty.
            
    • 
            
  • 
              Proof shape
              Induction on operations: the front is always the earliest still
              enqueued item.
            
    • 
            
  • 
              Assumes
              Plain FIFO — no priority reordering or stealing from the middle.

Proof techniques

Discrete math direct · contradiction · diagonalization mode direct

Grad algorithms and theory assume you can write proofs, not only follow them. Induction is one workhorse; this page covers the other everyday shapes—direct proof, contradiction, contrapositive—and a light touch of diagonalization used in decidability.

    Direct proof
    
        
    
          Assume the hypotheses, unfold definitions, and arrive at the conclusion.
          Example: if n is even, then n² is even — write n = 2k and expand.
        
    
        
    Contradiction & contrapositive
    
        
    
          Contradiction: assume the claim fails and derive absurdity (√2 irrational).
          Contrapositive: prove ¬Q ⇒ ¬P instead of P ⇒ Q — same truth table, often easier.
        
    
        
    Diagonalization
    
        
    
          List candidates in a table; build a new object that differs from row i in
          column i. Classic for “more reals than naturals” and for showing no
          Turing machine
          decides the halting problem.

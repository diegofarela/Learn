Expectation

Probability for CS average outcome · linearity E[S] = 3.5

Expectation is the probability-weighted average of a random variable. Linearity says you can split a sum even when terms depend on each other—the workhorse behind balls & bins analyses and hashing load. Builds on probability basics. Distinct from amortized analysis (deterministic averaging over a sequence of ops).

Die = sum of indicators

E[X]=3.5

Each face indicator Ik · roll to see X = Σ k·Ik

X = 1·I₁ + … + 6·I₆. E[X] = Σ k·(1/6) = 3.5 by linearity.

Definition

        

          For a discrete X taking values x with probabilities p(x),
          E[X] = Σ x p(x). It is not “the most likely value”—it is
          the center of mass of the distribution.
        


        
Linearity of expectation

        

          Always E[X+Y] = E[X]+E[Y], no independence required.
          Write complicated counts as sums of indicators, take E of each, add.
          That is how you get expected collisions without listing all pairs’
          dependence structure.

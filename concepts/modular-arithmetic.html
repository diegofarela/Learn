Modular arithmetic

Discrete math remainder · congruence · mod n 17 ≡ 5 (mod 12)

Modular arithmetic wraps integers when they pass the modulus—like hours on a clock. Congruences power hashing, bit tricks, crypto hashes (different “hash” goal than table buckets), and counting mod p. Not the same as floating-point remainder quirks in IEEE floats.

When this is useful

  • Clock math, hashing slots, and wrapping indexes in circular buffers.
  • Checksums, ISBN/credit-card check digits, and simple OTP-style codes.
  • Crypto and PRNG building blocks under the hood of secure tokens.

Congruence

        

          a ≡ b (mod n) means n divides (a−b). Equivalently, a and
          b leave the same remainder when divided by n—they land on the same
          clock tick.
        


        
Arithmetic wraps

        

          You can add and multiply residues: if a ≡ a′ and b ≡ b′ (mod n), then
          a+b ≡ a′+b′ and ab ≡ a′b′. That is why hash buckets and circular
          buffers stay consistent.

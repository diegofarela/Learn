Big-O complexity

Foundations growth rate · upper bound · asymptotics n = 16

Big-O answers one question: as the input grows, how does the work grow? It ignores machine speed and constant factors so you can compare searching and sorting by shape, not by milliseconds on one laptop.

What Big-O measures

        

          It measures how cost scales with input size n—usually
          time (steps) or space (memory). A linear scan of an array is
          O(n); nested loops over the same data are often
          O(n²). Big-O is an upper bound on growth for large
          n, not a stopwatch reading.

Best, average, worst

        

          The same algorithm can have different cases. Quicksort’s average is
          often O(n log n), but a bad pivot order can hit O(n²). Big-O usually
          quotes the worst case unless you say otherwise—so you
          know the ceiling when the input is adversarial.
        


        
Why constants drop

        

          Writing 3n + 100 as O(n) drops the 3 and the
          100. For huge n, the leading term dominates;
          constants and lower-order terms become noise in the growth story. They
          still matter in practice—just not for classifying the shape.

Breadth-first search

Algorithms layer by layer · FIFO frontier · O(V + E) visited 0/7

Breadth-first search (BFS) walks a graph (or tree) layer by layer: finish every node at distance d before touching distance d + 1. The frontier of “discovered but not yet expanded” nodes lives in a queue — first discovered, first expanded. Contrast with depth-first search, which dives deep along one branch before backtracking.

The FIFO frontier

        

          When you expand a node, its unseen neighbors join the
          rear of the queue. The next expansion always takes the
          front—so older discoveries (closer to the start) finish
          before newer ones. That is why BFS finds the shortest path in an
          unweighted graph: the first time you reach a node, you used the fewest
          edges. The demo’s frontier strip is the same
          queue
          ADT: enqueue at the rear, dequeue from the front.
